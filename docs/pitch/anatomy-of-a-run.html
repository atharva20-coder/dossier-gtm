A tool optimising for interesting would have opened with a paper on blood-pressure monitoring. This one dropped all six on age and wrote about the job he actually has.

Two more never reached the table at all. One fact belonged to a different person at Zamp; one belonged to a namesake the source never connected to the company. Both gates exist because both mistakes happened in testing, and an email built on either would have been confidently, unrecoverably wrong.

Where this run is wrong

The winning fact has no date, and that is why it won.

“Has been in the Founder's Office since January 2023” was dropped as stale at 45 months. “Joined Zamp as part of the Founder's Office” — the same event, without a date attached — scored highest. The ranking penalised the honest version for being specific about time.

The deeper version of the same problem: ranking still optimises for the most interesting fact rather than the one most connected to what the sender sells. On this run, “Zamp transitioned completely towards AI agents” and “already live at several F500 banks” are better reasons to start a conversation about research tooling than a role change from 2023 — but they are company-level, and the ranking prefers person-level on principle.

That is the next change, and it is a small one: the seller's own product already reaches the drafting stage, and it needs to reach the ranking stage too.
