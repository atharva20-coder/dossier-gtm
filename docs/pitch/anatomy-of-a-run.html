Three things to notice in that table

  • The six research papers. All real, all genuinely his, all about a decade old. A tool chasing the most interesting fact would have opened with blood-pressure monitoring. This one dropped all six on age and wrote about the job he actually has.
  • Two facts never even reached the table. One belonged to a colleague, one to a stranger with the same name. Both filters exist because both mistakes happened during testing — and an email built on either would have been confidently, unfixably wrong.
  • Nothing was deleted for being old. Old facts still appear, with their reason. They lose; they don't disappear.

Where this run got it wrong

Kept on the page deliberately. A walkthrough that only shows the good parts isn't a walkthrough.

The winning fact has no date — and that's why it won.

“Has been in the Founder's Office since January 2023” was dropped for being 45 months old. “Joined Zamp as part of the Founder's Office” — the same event, just without a date attached — came top.

So the ranking punished the more honest version for being specific about time.

The deeper version of the same problem

  • Ranking still chases the most interesting fact rather than the one most connected to what you're selling.
  • On this run, “Zamp transitioned completely towards AI agents” and “already live at several F500 banks” are better openers for a conversation about research tooling than a role change from 2023.
  • But those are about the company, and the ranking prefers facts about the person as a matter of principle.
  • The fix is small. What you sell already reaches the writing step. It needs to reach the ranking step too.
