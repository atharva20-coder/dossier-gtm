What to Say at Zamp

Two things make this different. Lead with those, and let the machinery come out in the questions.

The brief from Zamp's GTM head

Don't walk in and list features. Pitch the idea. There are two things that make this different from everything else in the category — say those, and let the mechanism come out in the questions.

Everything from step 05 onward in this document is an answer, not a script. Steps 01 to 04 are the pitch.

01

The problem, from watching the job

Open here. Not with the product, not with the stack — with the thing you found when you looked at how GTM people actually spend a day.

Say

“I went and looked at where GTM teams actually lose their time, and it isn't writing. It's the research before it. Scrolling the web, LinkedIn, X — jotting things into a doc. Then later, opening that doc again and trying to write a message out of it.”

Say

“That's two separate costs. Finding the signal, and then re-reading your own notes to turn it into an email. Both take real effort, and the second one is why people stop doing the first.”

Then land the consequence, because it is the reason the category exists and fails:

Say

“So most people skip it. They put the company name in a template and send it at scale, and the reply rate tells them exactly what the prospect thought of it.”

02

First thing that's different: it isn't a web search

This is the sentence to get right, because “AI researches your prospect” is what every tool in the category claims.

Say

“This isn't a web search with a nice wrapper. It goes to where a person actually leaves a trail — their LinkedIn, their posts on X — reads the pages, and validates every claim back against the source it came from. You get the complete view of the lead, not a summary of their company's homepage.”

Then the half people miss, which is about who you contact rather than what you send:

Say

“And it works the other way round too. Give it a company and it finds the handful of people there actually worth a conversation — instead of running a campaign at everyone in the org and hoping someone bites. Quality leads, not volume.”

Say it precisely

Don't say “I scrape LinkedIn” — you don't, and if they ask how you get past the login you'll be caught. Accurate, and the stronger answer — validation is the hard part, scraping isn't.

03

Second thing: the persona starts at fifteen percent, not zero

This is the one nobody else has. Spend the most time here.

Say

“Once the right leads are identified, it does the full research on each — who they are, what they've been posting on LinkedIn and X recently — and drafts the message. But the interesting part isn't the drafting.”

Say

“You design your own persona on day one. So you don't start at zero — you start at maybe fifteen percent, and the agent writes as you from the first message rather than sounding like a language model.”

Say

“Then it closes the gap by watching you. Every time you edit a draft before it goes out, it looks at what you changed, and after a few consistent edits it writes that down as a rule and applies it from the next message on. You never train it. You just keep editing — which you were going to do anyway. And that's the point: you stay in the loop, nothing sends by itself.”

If they ask whether that's real or a prompt trick, this is what's actually stored:

Show · Settings → Learned
  • Rules it inferred from your edits, each with how many edits it took.
  • It waits for three consistent edits — one edit is a mood, three is a preference.
  • Rules are corrected, not piled up: a new rule that contradicts an old one replaces it, and the set is capped.
  • A second list: which kinds of trigger you actually act on, learned from what you sent and hand-picked — not from drafts you left alone.
04

The GTM thinking underneath it

If they want to know how you decided what to build, this is the answer. Four levers, in order of impact on whether a meeting gets booked:

LeverWhy it ranks there
TargetingA perfect email to the wrong person fails every time
TimingIs there a reason to reach out this week?
Offer relevanceDoes what I sell connect to what they're dealing with?
PersonalisationThe craft of the sentence — fourth, not first
Say

“Personalisation is fourth on that list. Everyone builds for fourth. The research is what gets you the first three.”

And triggers are not equal. A new exec hired into the relevant function is the strongest signal there is — new leaders change vendors in their first ninety days. A job posting for roles next to your pain is next, and badly under-used. A funding round is high intent but saturated: everyone emails them that week. An award signals nothing about need.

Say

“Most tools treat a hiring post and a ‘best places to work' award as the same kind of signal. One is evidence of a problem. The other is a press release.”

05

The build, in plain steps

Walk the pipeline once, out loud, before touching the screen. Seven steps.

The pipeline
  • Resolve who they are. Two plausible people with the same name? It stops and asks, rather than researching a stranger.
  • Search outward. Tavily, anchored to the confirmed person — LinkedIn, X, press, podcasts.
  • Traverse. Follow threads the way a human would, but every query stays anchored to their name, so it can't drift onto their employer's competitors.
  • Extract candidate facts from the retrieved text.
  • Ground each one. The entities a fact claims must appear verbatim in the source. Deliberately dumb and deterministic — the model can't talk its way past a string match.
  • Judge. Score on recency, specificity, safety, and fit to what the writer sells. Pick one hook.
  • Draft in a saved persona's voice, then check the message actually used the hook's specific detail.
Say

“Every stage is written to the database as it happens, so if it breaks halfway you get a readable record instead of a spinner — and the screen shows you the reasoning, not just the answer.”

Have a finished lead open before you start talking. Never make them watch a loading state.

07

The three things that prove it's real

Anyone can demo a happy path. These are what to volunteer before you're asked, because each one is a bug found and fixed in the open.

It refuses to guess who someone is

A namesake's podcast appearance got picked as a hook for the wrong person. So there's a gate: a fact about someone who merely shares the name is dropped, and if two people are genuinely plausible the run stops and asks.

It won't take credit for someone else's news

An intern's award at the same company was nearly used as a hook. Now every extracted fact has to name whose it is, and one about a third party is dropped at every level.

It never dresses a guess up as a fact

When an address is calculated from a company's naming convention rather than looked up, it says derived — on screen, in the tooltip, and in the CSV a sequencer imports.

Say

“The verifier returned ‘valid' for an address I invented, because that domain accepts all mail. If I'd trusted it, the tool would have certified a fabricated address as confirmed. It now detects that and refuses to claim it.”

Handle carefully

Don't oversell the address finding. It's a free-tier email finder plus arithmetic, and it says so. Claiming verified data you don't have is exactly the failure the rest of the tool exists to avoid — and they'll spot it.

08

When they push back

“Why wouldn't a rep just use ChatGPT?”

Because it will invent the funding round. The whole system is the machinery that stops that: retrieval, verbatim grounding, and a refusal to write when there's nothing real to say.

“Does this actually get used in week three?”

Be honest: most tools like this don't, because reps live in Outreach and HubSpot and copy-pasting forty drafts is worse than not having the tool. That's why there's a CSV export with the columns a sequencer imports — and why the next thing I'd build is the CRM write-back, not more features.

“What would you do with real budget?”

A paid enrichment provider for verified contact data, and job-posting and exec-hire feeds — the two highest-intent triggers, which are the ones public search is worst at surfacing.

“What's it cost to run?”

Everything here is on free tiers, and I made the app respect them: nothing spends an allowance on a page load, only when a person asks for work. Every provider is optional — no key, wrong key, or out of quota all degrade to the same result, and the run tells you which happened.

If it breaks live

Say what happened and read the receipt off the screen. A tool that reports its own failure clearly is the point you've been making for twenty minutes — don't apologise for it, use it.

09

Close on the honest limitation

End on judgment, not features. It's the strongest note available and it can't be faked.

Say

“The thing I'd fix first isn't in the interface. It's that ranking still leans toward the most interesting fact rather than the one that best connects to what you sell. I know exactly where that change goes, and it's a small change with a large effect on quality. That's what I'd do on day one.”

Read the problem before the product. Show the reasoning, not just the answer. Volunteer the flaw before it's found.
