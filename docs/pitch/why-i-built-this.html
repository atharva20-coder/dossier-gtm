PS-3 · GTM outreach

Why I Built This

GTM teams don’t lose their time on the outreach. They lose it on the research before it — and that’s the part everyone skips.

01

What did you build? Which problem did you select?

I chose to work upon the PS3 — GTM outreach problem.

02

Why did you choose this?

While working at Axis with Sarvam, Gnani, Greylabs, and at ArisX with Bolna, I met a lot of GTMs.

While working at Axis and ArisX, I always got messages and mails from GTMs reaching out to me for their service — but the message always looked irrelevant, disengaging, and most importantly, looked like someone just sent it as a formality to a bulk list of people they could send it to.

When I got the problem statement from Palak, I read the document and this problem immediately struck me, so I started digging into it.

I went and looked at where GTM teams actually lose their time. I spoke with some of the GTMs I know at companies like Sarvam, Greylabs and Bolna, and one common thing came out of those conversations:

What they all said

The GTM teams lose their time, and it isn’t outreach — it’s the research before it.

Scrolling the web, LinkedIn, X — jotting things down in a doc one by one, and then later opening the doc again and trying to write a message out of it by selecting your hooks from the doc.

That’s two separate costs: finding the signal, and then reading your own notes to turn it into an email. Both take real effort, so most people skip it. They put the company name in a template and send it at scale.

03

What is Dossier, and how is it different?

Dossier isn’t a simple web search with a nice wrapper. It goes where a person actually leaves a trail — their LinkedIn, their posts on X — reads the pages, and checks every claim back against the page it came from. You get a real view of the lead, not a summary of their company’s homepage.

And it works the other way round too. Give it a company, and it finds the handful of people there actually worth a conversation — instead of running a campaign at everyone in the org and hoping someone bites.

04

The second difference: a persona that learns you

Once the right leads are identified, it researches each one properly — who they are, what they’ve been posting on LinkedIn and X recently — and drafts the message. But the interesting part isn’t drafting.

It’s the self-learning persona. You design your persona on day one, so you don’t start at zero — you start at one, and it writes as you from the very first message, rather than sounding like a language model.

Now the interesting part — the self-learning

The agent lives inside the application, and it closes the gap by watching you. Every time you edit a draft before sending — change the hooks, change the writing style — it looks at what you changed. After a few consistent edits, it writes down the rule and applies it from the next message it drafts. You never train it. You just keep editing, until it learns you — and that’s the point.

How it learns. It’s not a clever prompt, it’s a system. The agent waits for three confident and consistent changes, then scores it.
